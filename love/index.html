A Mathematical Framework for Relational Tile Dynamics

from a place within the heart

this is based on a presentation i made for a friend after his breakup. i apologize in advance for the quality of the work: it was made in a night, with someone crying right next to me! haha

Motivation

  • Human beings are modelled as tileable regions encoding capacities, values, needs, and fulfillment.
  • Missing aspects of self correspond to holes in these tilings.
  • Relationships create transformations that fill, move, or reshape these holes.
  • We seek a clean mathematical language for:
    • individuals as structured sets,
    • missing pieces as holes,
    • relationships as dynamical tile-exchange processes,
    • memory and anticipation as operators acting on time,
    • global external influences and individual effort.

Individuals as Tileable Regions

Let each person be represented by a region

\[ A \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n \]

together with a tiling

\[ \mathcal{T}_A = \{T_{A,i}\}_{i \in I_A} \]

such that

\[ A = \bigcup_{i \in I_A} T_{A,i}, \qquad \operatorname{int}(T_{A,i}) \cap \operatorname{int}(T_{A,j}) = \varnothing. \]

Interpretation:

  • Tiles represent relatively stable “fulfilled aspects” of the person.
  • Boundaries encode tensions, limits, or openness to change.

Missing Aspects as Holes

A missing piece (unmet need, unresolved history, unrealized facet) is encoded as a hole

\[ \alpha_A \subseteq A \]

which is not covered by any tile:

\[ \mathcal{T}_A^{(-\alpha)} := \mathcal{T}_A \setminus \{\alpha_A\}. \]

The “incomplete person-state” is the pair

\[ (A,\ \mathcal{T}_A^{(-\alpha)}). \]

Holes may be:

  • structural (long-term patterns),
  • situational (context-dependent),
  • relational (defined relative to particular others).

Relational Transformations

A relationship between \(A\) and \(B\) induces transformations

\[ f_i : \mathcal{T}_A^{(-\alpha)} \times \mathcal{T}_B^{(-\beta)} \longrightarrow \mathcal{T}_A^{(-\alpha_i)} \times \mathcal{T}_B^{(-\beta_i)}. \]

Interpretation:

  • \(f_i\) fills or reshapes one missing aspect while possibly exposing another.
  • Both individuals may be changed, asymmetrically or symmetrically.
  • \(f_i\) may encode conversation, conflict, care, rupture, repair, etc.

Temporal Structure: Periods of Interaction

Each relational event has a temporal support:

\[ P_i = [t_i^-, t_i^+] \subset \mathbb{R}. \]

A sequence of relational events is

\[ (f_1, P_1),\ (f_2, P_2),\ \ldots,\ (f_k, P_k). \]

We can define a cumulative relational dynamic

\[ \mathcal{F}_{A,B}(t) = f_k \circ f_{k-1} \circ \cdots \circ f_1, \]

where \(k\) is such that \(t \in P_k\) or \(t\) lies after the \(k\)-th interaction.

  • At each stage, new holes appear or old ones are resolved.
  • The interior tilings of \(A\) and \(B\) evolve as a dynamical system.

Global Interaction Field: A’s Life with Everything

Beyond any specific relationship, each person \(A\) interacts with many people and situations. Let \(x_A(t)\) denote the state of \(A\) in some configuration space (emotional, cognitive, relational).

\[ x_A(t) \in \mathcal{X}. \]

Let \(\mathcal{F}_A\) be the set of external influences (pulls) on \(A\):

\[ \mathcal{F}_A = \{f_{A,1}, f_{A,2}, f_{A,3}, \ldots\}. \]

Then schematically, the net external pull is

\[ \frac{dx_A}{dt}(t) \approx \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_A} f(t), \]

where each \(f\) encodes the effect of some person, place, event, or environment on \(A\).

Specific Path: The A–B Relationship Inside the Field

For a given relationship between \(A\) and \(B\), we distinguish:

  • a relationship-specific pull \(f_{A,B}(t)\) on \(A\) due to \(B\),
  • the residual set of pulls \(\mathcal{F}_A \setminus \{f_{A,B}\}\) from everything else.

Then we can decompose:

\[ \frac{dx_A}{dt}(t) = f_{A,B}(t) + \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_A \setminus \{f_{A,B}\}} f(t), \]

and similarly for \(B\):

\[ \frac{dx_B}{dt}(t) = f_{B,A}(t) + \sum_{g \in \mathcal{F}_B \setminus \{f_{B,A}\}} g(t). \]

The global path of \(A\) or \(B\) is therefore different from the hypothetical path where only the \(A\)–\(B\) relationship exists.

Memory Operators: Retrospective vs Prospective

Each person \(A\) carries an internal evaluation system decomposed into:

\[ C_A = \left( C_A^{\mathrm{ret}},\ C_A^{\mathrm{pro}} \right). \]
  • Retrospective evaluation (looking back):
    \[ C_A^{\mathrm{ret}} : \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{past}} \longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_A, \]
    where \(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{past}}\) is the set of past time-intervals and \(\mathcal{M}_A\) is the space of internal “memory states” of \(A\).
  • Prospective projection (looking forward):
    \[ C_A^{\mathrm{pro}} : B \times \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{future}} \longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_A^{\mathrm{pred}}, \]
    encoding how \(A\) projects possible futures with \(B\) on a future interval.

Retrospective evaluations are based on a completed trajectory; prospective projections are based on partial information and are inherently uncertain.

Retrospective Evaluation: Complete Trajectories

For a past interaction period \(P \subset (0, t)\), the relational dynamics

\[ \{f_i\}_{i :\, P_i \subseteq P} \]

and resulting tilings are fully determined. Then

\[ C_A^{\mathrm{ret}}(P) \]

is a (deterministic) function of the realized history on \(P\):

\[ C_A^{\mathrm{ret}}(P) = \Phi_A\big( \{(f_i, P_i) : P_i \subseteq P\},\ (A, \mathcal{T}_A),\ (B, \mathcal{T}_B) \big), \]

for some evaluation functional \(\Phi_A\).

  • This captures how \(A\) remembers, summarizes, or re-narrates a completed episode.
  • No stochasticity is needed once the underlying trajectory is fixed.

Perception of B and Coverage Fraction

\(A\) does not directly access the full internal tiling of \(B\):

\[ (B, \mathcal{T}_B). \]

Instead, \(A\) has a perceived representation

\[ \widehat{B}_A = \Pi_A(B, \mathcal{T}_B), \]

where \(\Pi_A\) is a projection/observation map (filters, blind spots, ideals, etc.).

We define a coverage fraction

\[ \kappa_A(B) \in (0, 1] \]

measuring how much of \(B\)’s interior \(A\) has effectively encountered:

\[ \kappa_A(B) \approx \frac{\text{number of tiles of } B \text{ that } A \text{ has interacted with}}{\text{effective total number of tiles of } B}. \]
  • \(\kappa_A(B)\) is high if \(A\) has seen \(B\) in many contexts.
  • Low \(\kappa_A(B)\) means \(A\) is predicting from a very partial picture.

History-Dependent Information: \(H_{A,B}\)

Let \(H_{A,B}(t)\) be the relational history up to time \(t\):

\[ H_{A,B}(t) := \{ (f_i, P_i) : P_i \subset (-\infty, t],\ A \text{ and } B \text{ both involved} \}. \]

Together with the coverage fraction \(\kappa_A(B)\), this describes what \(A\) has to work with when making predictions:

\[ I_A(B, t) := \big( H_{A,B}(t),\ \kappa_A(B) \big). \]
  • \(I_A(B, t)\) is the information state of \(A\) about \(B\) at time \(t\).
  • Retrospective evaluations \(C_A^{\mathrm{ret}}\) are based on realized parts of \(H_{A,B}(t)\).
  • Prospective projections \(C_A^{\mathrm{pro}}\) use \(I_A(B, t)\) to form beliefs about future \(f_i\).

Prospective Projection as a Stochastic Operator

We now introduce a stochastic model for future interactions. Let \((\Omega, \mathcal{F}, \mathbb{P})\) be a probability space. For a future interval

\[ I \subset (t, \infty), \]

define a random future relational transformation

\[ F_I : \Omega \longrightarrow \mathcal{F}_{A,B} \]

whose distribution depends on \(I_A(B, t)\):

\[ \mathbb{P}\big( F_I \in \cdot \mid I_A(B, t) \big). \]

Let \(U_A\) be an evaluation functional that maps a future trajectory into an internal valuation (e.g., emotional payoff, stability, risk):

\[ U_A : \mathcal{F}_{A,B} \longrightarrow \mathcal{M}_A^{\mathrm{pred}}. \]

Then the prospective operator is naturally defined as a conditional expectation:

\[ C_A^{\mathrm{pro}}(B, I) := \mathbb{E}\big[ U_A(F_I) \mid I_A(B, t) \big]. \]

Accuracy Gap: Retrospective vs Prospective

Retrospective and prospective evaluations differ in their information content:

  • Retrospective: \(C_A^{\mathrm{ret}}(P)\) is a (nearly) deterministic function of a completed history on \(P\).
  • Prospective: \(C_A^{\mathrm{pro}}(B, I)\) is an expectation over unknown future transformations \(F_I\).

A simple way to encode the idea that predictions are “less accurate”:

\[ \operatorname{Var}\big( C_A^{\mathrm{pro}}(B, I) \big) \propto \frac{1}{\kappa_A(B)}. \]
  • As \(\kappa_A(B) \to 1\) (high coverage), prediction variance shrinks.
  • As \(\kappa_A(B) \to 0\) (little knowledge), prediction variance is large.
  • Retrospective evaluations correspond (in this picture) to the limit of zero variance once the path is realized.

Effort Toward a Relationship

We now model the intentional effort each person invests in the \(A\)–\(B\) relationship.

  • \(E_A \ge 0\): effort of \(A\) directed toward maintaining or strengthening the \(A\)–\(B\) path.
  • \(E_B \ge 0\): effort of \(B\) directed toward \(A\).

These are not just “forces” but resource-like quantities:

  • time, attention, emotional labor,
  • willingness to change tiles / reshape holes for the other,
  • active resistance against dispersive influences pulling them apart.

Dispersive Influences from Everything Else

Among the global influences \(\mathcal{F}_A\), we separate those that tend to pull \(A\) away from \(B\):

\[ \mathcal{F}_A^{\mathrm{away}} \subseteq \mathcal{F}_A, \qquad \mathcal{F}_B^{\mathrm{away}} \subseteq \mathcal{F}_B. \]

Define scalar dispersive pressures

\[ D_A := \sum_{f \in \mathcal{F}_A^{\mathrm{away}}} \|f\|, \qquad D_B := \sum_{g \in \mathcal{F}_B^{\mathrm{away}}} \|g\|. \]

Interpretation:

  • \(D_A\) is the total “pull” of everything else trying to redirect \(A\)’s trajectory away from the \(A\)–\(B\) path.
  • \(D_B\) is the analogous quantity for \(B\).
  • These may come from other people, commitments, environments, or internal conflicts.

A Simple Stability Inequality

A minimal condition for the \(A\)–\(B\) relationship to be net pulled together rather than apart is:

\[ E_A + E_B \ge D_A + D_B. \]

Equivalently, with

\[ E_{AB} := E_A + E_B, \qquad D_{AB} := D_A + D_B, \]

we require

\[ E_{AB} \ge D_{AB}. \]

Interpretation:

  • The combined effort toward the relationship outweighs the combined dispersive pressures.
  • If \(E_{AB} < D_{AB}\), the net drift is away from a shared path, even if one side is trying hard.
  • This leaves room for asymmetry (e.g., \(E_A \gg E_B\)) while focusing on the joint balance of forces.

Directional Dynamics and Noise (Sketch)

We can focus on the component of motion along the \(A\)–\(B\) direction in state space. Let \(x_{AB}(t)\) be a scalar measuring “closeness” or alignment along the shared path. Then a stylized stochastic dynamics is

\[ dx_{AB}(t) = \big( E_A^{\parallel}(t) + E_B^{\parallel}(t) - D_A^{\parallel}(t) - D_B^{\parallel}(t) \big)\, dt + \sigma_{AB}\, dW_t, \]

where:

  • \(E_A^{\parallel}, E_B^{\parallel}\) are the components of effort pulling along the \(A\)–\(B\) direction,
  • \(D_A^{\parallel}, D_B^{\parallel}\) are the components of dispersive pulls along that direction,
  • \(dW_t\) is Brownian noise capturing unpredictability and random shocks.

In expectation, a convergence bias requires

\[ \mathbb{E}\big[ E_A^{\parallel} + E_B^{\parallel} \big] \ge \mathbb{E}\big[ D_A^{\parallel} + D_B^{\parallel} \big]. \]

Relational Tile Dynamics: Extended View

A full relational system between \(A\) and \(B\) can be summarized as

\[ \mathcal{R}_{A,B} = \big( A,\ \mathcal{T}_A,\ B,\ \mathcal{T}_B,\ \{(f_i, P_i)\},\ C_A^{\mathrm{ret}},\ C_A^{\mathrm{pro}},\ C_B^{\mathrm{ret}},\ C_B^{\mathrm{pro}} \big) \]
\[ \big( \kappa_A(B),\ \kappa_B(A),\ E_A,\ E_B,\ D_A,\ D_B \big). \]

This form makes it clear how:

  • global forces interact with intentional effort,
  • memory and prediction shape the \(A\)–\(B\) trajectory,
  • coverage fractions determine informational accuracy,
  • convergence or drift occurs depending on \(E_{AB}\) vs \(D_{AB}\).

For you

Dear you,

A Least-Action Promise

Let \(x(t)\) be my path through the world, and let \(x_{\text{you}}\) be the point in state space that means by your side. For \(t \in [t_0, T]\), define the action

\[ \mathcal{A}[x] = \int_{t_0}^{T} \left( \frac{1}{2} \|\dot{x}(t)\|^2 + D(t) - E_{\text{us}}(t) + V_{\text{you}}(x(t)) \right) dt, \]

where:

  • \(D(t)\) collects all dispersive pulls trying to pull me away,
  • \(E_{\text{us}}(t)\) is the effort I choose to invest in us,
  • \(V_{\text{you}}(x)\) is a potential well centered at \(x_{\text{you}}\).

My promise is to choose the trajectory

\[ x^*(t) = \arg\min_{x(t)} \left\{ \mathcal{A}[x] \;\middle|\; x(t_0) \text{ given},\ x(T) = x_{\text{you}} \right\}. \]

Among all the lives I could live, I choose the path of least action that ends with me beside you.
